Part One

What is Umrah?

A "lesser pilgrimage" to the Ka'bah performed at any time of year, made up of a small, fixed set of rites.

Linguistically, ʿumrah (عمرة) comes from iʿtimār, "to visit a populated/frequented place" — a name for visiting the Ka'bah.

In Sharia, it is the worship of visiting the Sacred House (Ka'bah) to perform ṭawāf (circling it seven times) and saʿī (walking between Ṣafā and Marwah seven times) while in the state of iḥrām, then shaving or trimming the hair — with no fixed date, no standing at ʿArafah, and no stoning of the pillars, which belong to Ḥajj alone.

Distinguishing feature vs. Ḥajj: Umrah can be performed at any time of the year except that most scholars discourage combining it with Hajj days for non-pilgrims. Ref: Qur'an 2:196 mentions Ḥajj and ʿUmrah together as two related but distinct acts of worship.

Part Two

Why We Perform It

The rites of Umrah re-enact a true story of trust in Allah — scroll to follow it.

Ibrāhīm عليه السلام, commanded by Allah, brought his wife Hājar and their infant son Ismāʿīl عليهما السلام to a barren valley with no water, no crop, and no people — the future site of Makkah.

He left them there and turned to go. Hājar followed him, asking again and again, "O Ibrāhīm, where are you going, leaving us in this valley with no one and nothing?" He did not answer. She asked, "Did Allah command you to do this?" He said, "Yes." She replied, "Then He will not neglect us," and let him go.

Source: Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 3364 (the long narration of the settling of Makkah, from Ibn ʿAbbās).

When the water ran out and Ismāʿīl began to cry from thirst, Hājar ran between the two hills of Ṣafā and Marwah seven times, searching desperately for help or water, straining to see any traveler in the distance.

On her return she found water gushing from beneath her infant's feet — Zamzam. She cupped the earth around it with her hands and said "Zam zam" ("stop, stop" / "gather, gather"), containing the spring. Had she left it flowing, the Prophet ﷺ said, it would have been a flowing river.

Source: Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 3364.

Travelers saw birds circling the spring and came to settle nearby — this became the population of Makkah, and the Jurhum tribe grew up around Hājar and Ismāʿīl. Later, Ibrāhīm and Ismāʿīl together raised the foundations of the Ka'bah at Allah's command.

Source: Qur'an 2:127; Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 3364.

Every Umrah we perform walks Hājar's path exactly: seven circuits between Ṣafā and Marwah, and we drink from the same Zamzam. The rites are not symbolic decoration — they are a re-enactment of her tested trust in Allah, preserved for every generation of pilgrims after her.

Step 1 — Iḥrām

The sacred state you enter before crossing the miqāt, marking the start of Umrah. It is a state of intention, dress, and restraint — not just clothing.

  1. Trim nails, remove underarm/pubic hair, perform ghusl (or wuḍūʾ if ghusl isn't possible) before the miqāt.
  2. Men wear two unstitched white sheets (izār around the waist, ridāʾ over the shoulders) and sandals that leave the ankles/top of the foot uncovered. Women wear their normal modest, non-adorned clothing — the face and hands stay uncovered from a niqāb or gloves, though covering the face with a loose cloth (not touching the skin) in front of non-maḥram men is permitted by many scholars.
  3. Pray 2 rakʿahs if the timing allows (not obligatory).
  4. Make the intention (niyyah) specifically for ʿUmrah, then say the Talbiyah — this is the moment iḥrām formally begins.
  5. Keep repeating the Talbiyah frequently until you begin ṭawāf.
Niyyah (Intention)

اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أُرِيدُ الْعُمْرَةَ فَيَسِّرْهَا لِي وَتَقَبَّلْهَا مِنِّي

Allāhumma innī urīdu al-ʿumrata fa-yassirhā lī wa taqabbalhā minnī.

"O Allah, I intend to perform ʿUmrah — make it easy for me and accept it from me."

The niyyah is an act of the heart and does not require specific words; this is a commonly used scholarly phrasing, not a verbatim Prophetic narration.

Talbiyah

لَبَّيْكَ اللَّهُمَّ لَبَّيْكَ، لَبَّيْكَ لَا شَرِيكَ لَكَ لَبَّيْكَ، إِنَّ الْحَمْدَ وَالنِّعْمَةَ لَكَ وَالْمُلْكَ، لَا شَرِيكَ لَكَ

Labbayka Allāhumma labbayk, labbayka lā sharīka laka labbayk, inna al-ḥamda wa'n-niʿmata laka wa'l-mulk, lā sharīka lak.

"Here I am, O Allah, here I am. Here I am, You have no partner, here I am. Indeed all praise, favor, and sovereignty are Yours. You have no partner."

Source: Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 1549, Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 1184.

⚠ Common mistakes

  • Crossing the miqāt boundary without having entered iḥrām (a fidyah may be owed — see the rules below).
  • Men wearing stitched garments or covering the head; using scented soap/perfume after the niyyah.
  • Women wearing a face-covering niqāb or gloves during iḥrām.
  • Trimming nails or hair after the niyyah has been made.

Step 2 — Ṭawāf al-ʿUmrah

Seven circuits of the Ka'bah, counter-clockwise, with the Ka'bah kept on your left shoulder, starting and ending level with the Black Stone (al-Ḥajar al-Aswad).

  1. Align yourself with the Black Stone corner. If you can reach it, touch/kiss it; if not, point/gesture toward it and say "Allāhu akbar" — do not push or harm anyone.
  2. Men perform iḍṭibāʿ (baring the right shoulder by passing the ridāʾ under the right arm) for all 7 circuits, and ramal (brisk, short steps) for the first 3 circuits only — both Sunnah for men, specific to this first ṭawāf.
  3. Keep the Ka'bah on your left the whole way round; walk outside the low semi-circular wall (the Ḥijr Ismāʿīl) — passing inside it skips part of the Ka'bah's actual footprint and invalidates that circuit.
  4. Pass the Yamānī Corner (no need to touch it if crowded) and recite the du'a below on the final stretch back to the Black Stone. Repeat for all 7 circuits.
  5. After 7 circuits, pray 2 rakʿahs, ideally behind Maqām Ibrāhīm (if not possible due to crowding, anywhere in the Ḥaram is valid), then drink Zamzam.
Between the Yamānī Corner and the Black Stone

رَبَّنَا آتِنَا فِي الدُّنْيَا حَسَنَةً وَفِي الْآخِرَةِ حَسَنَةً وَقِنَا عَذَابَ النَّارِ

Rabbanā ātinā fi'd-dunyā ḥasanatan wa fi'l-ākhirati ḥasanatan wa qinā ʿadhāba'n-nār.

"Our Lord, give us good in this world and good in the Hereafter, and protect us from the punishment of the Fire."

Source: Qur'an 2:201; narrated as the Prophet ﷺ's regular du'a on this stretch of ṭawāf in Sunan Abī Dāwūd 1892.

Note: there is no fixed, authentically-narrated du'a for each individual circuit of ṭawāf. Popular "seven du'as, one per circuit" booklets are a later compilation, not something the Prophet ﷺ specified. Recite Qur'an, dhikr, or personal supplication freely throughout — in any language.

If you are menstruating: the majority view (Mālikī, Shāfiʿī, Ḥanbalī) is that ṭawāf requires purity exactly like ṣalāh — a menstruating woman does everything else a pilgrim does (talbiyah, standing, remembrance) but must wait until she is pure to perform ṭawāf, based on the Prophet ﷺ's words to ʿĀʾishah: "Do everything the pilgrim does, except do not perform ṭawāf around the House until you become pure" (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 305, Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 1211). The Ḥanafī school agrees purity is required in principle, but permits a menstruating woman who genuinely cannot delay her departure to perform ṭawāf regardless, owing a dam (sacrifice) as expiation — a concession scholars in this school extend from the rules of Ḥajj. If your schedule allows waiting even a day or two, plan around it; if not, ask a local scholar about your specific case before departure.

⚠ Common mistakes

  • Starting the count from the wrong point (must be level with the Black Stone corner, not the door of the Ka'bah).
  • Cutting through the Ḥijr Ismāʿīl instead of walking around it.
  • Fighting the crowd to touch the Black Stone — a gesture from a distance is fully sufficient.
  • Losing count in a crowd — use the counter below.

Step 3 — Saʿī

Seven lengths walked between the hills of Ṣafā and Marwah, re-enacting Hājar's search for water — starting at Ṣafā (length 1 ends at Marwah, length 2 returns to Ṣafā, and so on; length 7 ends at Marwah).

  1. Ascend Ṣafā, face the Ka'bah's direction, and recite the takbīr and dhikr below (three times, with personal du'a between each).
  2. Walk to Marwah. Men jog gently through the green-lit zone marked by the green fluorescent lights (a Sunnah for men only — women walk normally throughout).
  3. Ascend Marwah and repeat the dhikr, facing the Ka'bah's direction.
  4. Continue back and forth — each one-way trip is one length — until 7 lengths are complete, ending at Marwah.
At Ṣafā and Marwah (recite atop each, 3×)

لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَحْدَهُ لَا شَرِيكَ لَهُ، لَهُ الْمُلْكُ وَلَهُ الْحَمْدُ، وَهُوَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ، لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَحْدَهُ، أَنْجَزَ وَعْدَهُ، وَنَصَرَ عَبْدَهُ، وَهَزَمَ الْأَحْزَابَ وَحْدَهُ

Lā ilāha illallāhu waḥdahu lā sharīka lah, lahu'l-mulku wa lahu'l-ḥamd, wa huwa ʿalā kulli shay'in qadīr. Lā ilāha illallāhu waḥdah, anjaza waʿdah, wa naṣara ʿabdah, wa hazama'l-aḥzāba waḥdah.

"There is no god but Allah alone, with no partner. His is the dominion and His is all praise, and He is capable of all things. There is no god but Allah alone, who fulfilled His promise, granted victory to His slave, and defeated the confederates alone."

Source: Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 1218 (Jābir's long narration describing the Prophet ﷺ's Ḥajj).

⚠ Common mistakes

  • Starting at Marwah instead of Ṣafā — this invalidates the length; you must restart at Ṣafā.
  • Losing count of lengths in a crowd — remember, unlike ṭawāf, the finish alternates ends each length.
  • Assuming the jog (green zone) is required for women — it is not; women walk normally throughout.

Step 4 — Ḥalq or Taqṣīr

Shaving or trimming the hair completes Umrah and lifts the restrictions of iḥrām.

  1. Men either shave the entire head (ḥalq — more virtuous, the Prophet ﷺ prayed for shavers three times and trimmers once) or trim it evenly all over (taqṣīr, at least a fingertip's length from all sides — not just the fringe).
  2. Women only trim — gathering the hair and cutting off roughly a fingertip's length from the ends. Women do not shave their heads.
  3. Once this is done, iḥrām ends and everything that was prohibited (normal clothing, perfume, marital relations, cutting hair/nails) becomes permissible again — your Umrah is complete.

Source: Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 1727, Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 1301 (du'a for those who shave); women's trimming is established by consensus (ijmāʿ) and narrated in Sunan Abī Dāwūd.

⚠ Common mistakes

  • Women shaving the head — not permitted; trimming only.
  • Cutting only a symbolic snip of a few strands rather than a fingertip's length from around the whole head.
  • Doing ḥalq/taqṣīr before finishing all 7 lengths of saʿī.
  • Forgetting this step counts as still being in iḥrām — restrictions remain until it's done.

Sources policy

Every factual claim about the rites is referenced to the Qur'an (surah:verse) or to hadith — primarily Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. Where a narration comes from the Sunan collections (Abū Dāwūd, al-Tirmidhī, Ibn Mājah, al-Nasāʾī), it is used only where widely graded ṣaḥīḥ or ḥasan, and any scholarly disagreement on grading is noted rather than hidden. Where madhhabs differ — such as the ruling on Umrah's obligation, or ṭawāf during menstruation — every view is named rather than picking one silently. Where no authentic specific wording exists for a popular practice (e.g. a fixed du'a per ṭawāf circuit), this guide says so explicitly instead of inventing one. This is a study and companion tool, not a substitute for a qualified local scholar — please verify anything consequential before acting on it.
